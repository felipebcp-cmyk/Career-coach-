**1. Why do stories hold attention where raw information doesn't?**

- Stories are shorter and easier to remember than facts
- **Attention evolved to track uncertain outcomes that matter — and story is exactly that packaging** ✓
- People are less intelligent than they used to be
- Information is inherently boring

> The brain budgets attention for stakes — consequential, unresolved outcomes. Story is the format built around stakes; a fact list has none.

**2. As machines absorb more utility work, the lasting premium shifts toward:**

- **Meaning, identity and experience — the felt, human part of value** ✓
- Lower prices across every category
- Longer, more detailed product documentation
- Whoever can do utility slightly faster

> Utility is wanted invisible and cheap. What people freely overpay for — restaurants, brands, transformation — is meaning and identity, which automation doesn't supply.

> Modern survival tension is financial, social and opportunistic — threats to what you depend on. Falling behind registers as danger; preferences and hobbies don't.

**4. 'Problem first, always' works because:**
