Module 2 — From Analysis to Influence · Lesson 3 of 5 · ~20 min read

Translate, don't simplify down

Non-finance leaders aren't less intelligent — they're fluent in a different language. 'EBITDA margin compression from adverse mix' becomes 'we're selling more of the products we make less money on'. Keep the substance, change the vocabulary. If you must use a finance term, define it in the same breath, once.

Adapt to the person, not just the topic

People consume information differently. Some want the detail and the workings (analytical); some want the headline and the action (driver); some want to know who's affected (relational); some want the vision and the story (expressive). Watch how a stakeholder communicates and mirror it: the driver gets one page with a recommendation up top; the analytical gets the model and the assumptions.

Choose the channel deliberately

  • Bad news or challenge: face-to-face (or video) first, never email first.
  • Complex analysis: pre-read plus a short walkthrough, not a live data dump.
  • Quick decisions: one-pager with a clear recommendation and options.
  • Routine updates: consistent dashboard, same shape every time so change stands out.

Check for landing

Communication happened only if it was received. Close conversations by asking what the other person will take away or do next. If their summary doesn't match your message, that's your rework, not their failure.

Key takeaways

  • Translate finance language into business language without losing substance.
  • Match style to the person: detail for analyticals, headlines for drivers.
  • Bad news travels in person first; complex analysis travels as pre-read plus walkthrough.
  • Confirm what landed — the message is what they heard, not what you said.
